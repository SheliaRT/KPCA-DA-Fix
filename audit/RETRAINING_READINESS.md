## Data correction and current protocol

The prior `Data/TvsN/GSE45498_NvT.csv` must not be used for model evaluation. Its 183 expression profiles all match NCBI's official GSE45498 supplementary matrix, but matching those profiles to the GPL16299 Series Matrix reveals 52 incorrect stored labels and duplicated sample names that assign some profiles to the wrong patient. This invalidates all held-out results made with that target, including the seed-43 through seed-45 runs. They remain in `results/` for audit history only and must not be cited as performance evidence.

[`rebuild_gse45498_tvsn.py`](rebuild_gse45498_tvsn.py) reconstructs the target from the downloaded official NCBI files. It matches each expression profile to its GEO accession/title, derives labels and numeric patient IDs from GEO metadata, and excludes metastatic specimens. Its output, `Data/TvsN/GSE45498_TvsN_official_200.csv`, contains 236 genes, 160 Tumor and 40 Normal samples from 158 patient groups. The sample-level provenance, source URLs and hashes are in [gse45498_tvsn_rebuild_manifest.json](gse45498_tvsn_rebuild_manifest.json). A new [GSE41970/GSE45498 mRNA crosswalk](gse41970_mrna_crosswalk.json) confirms both accessions contain identical mRNA supplements and sample metadata. The 224-sample count in the original article refers to the 165-tumor/59-normal miRNA analysis; its mRNA analysis reports 158 tumors, 40 normals and 54 metastases. The available public GPL16299 matrix has 160 tumors, 40 normals and 54 metastases, so the usable mRNA TvsN subset is 200—not 224. The exact two primary tumor samples excluded from the article's mRNA analysis are not identified in GEO metadata; do not guess which to remove. The second GEO supplement contains only `nmiR` microRNA features, as documented in [gse45498_raw_data_audit.json](gse45498_raw_data_audit.json), and cannot resolve the two-sample difference. The corrected local file is the most complete reproducible public mRNA target available, with the remaining two-case source-publication mismatch clearly bounded.

The experiment runner and readiness validator now default to `TCGA_BRCA_NvT_new.csv`, excluding its two `Unknown` sample-type-06 rows, and the reconstructed GEO target. The refreshed readiness artifact now records 15,743 retained source genes, 232 retained target genes and 222 shared genes under the deterministic variance filter. This changes the prior source-feature count by one while leaving the target/shared counts unchanged. The filter discards `floor(0.30 × feature_count)` lowest-variance genes by stable rank, removes constants, and fits target-private filtering on adaptation samples only. The source candidate is closer to the paper's 592 samples; its inclusion/provenance policy still needs to be recorded.

The patient-grouped split assigns 471/118 source rows to train/validation and 139/61 target rows to adaptation/test, with no patient crossing partitions. It performs no KPCA fitting or classifier training. Under the deterministic rank filter, 16.11% of target-adaptation retained values (16.82% of shared-gene values) fall outside `[0,1]`, with maximum 50.70. A seed-52 adaptation-only scaling diagnostic confirmed the shared-gene gap: 18.28% are outside the source-fitted range, maximum 50.70, p99 4.77; fitting on the adaptation rows alone maps those rows to `[0,1]` and gives median target/source per-gene range ratio 2.20. It used no target labels or held-out values. This supports testing adaptation-fitted shared scaling as a separate, label-free preprocessing candidate; it is not performance evidence, so retain source-fitted scaling until a matched classifier comparison supports a change. Clipping remains a separate matched ablation, not an assumed fix.

The image path preserves float intensities through `ToTensor`; it does not divide them by 255. The seed-51 raw-map preflight found 16.76% of held-out target shared-gene values outside `[0,1]`, with a maximum of 26.276, corresponding to ImageNet-normalized values as high as 115.27. The runner now records actual image and normalized input ranges in preflight/run artifacts. For label-safe development, pass `--withhold-target-test-labels`; the run saves predictions without `y_true`, and `audit/score_withheld_target_run.py` provides a hash- and split-checked one-time score after a candidate is frozen. Current clipped/unclipped held-out comparisons are exploratory and cannot determine the final intensity policy.

A detached input-reader preflight also evaluated a numeric-first CSV parser. It preserved the legacy loader's outputs exactly on a synthetic fixture, read the full TCGA candidate (22,489 genes × 591 samples) in 5.19 seconds, and read the reconstructed GEO matrix (236 genes × 200 samples) in 0.021 seconds while returning no target label array. A clean detached parity check matched the full corrected-GEO expression values, gene order, sample IDs and labels exactly; its unlabeled mode returned no label array. The same check matched the legacy loader on a 64-gene TCGA-format fixture across all 591 sample columns. These timings are for the candidate parser only, and full TCGA parity plus an apples-to-apples timing remain outstanding, so the production loader has not been changed.

A seed-46, label-free scaling diagnostic on the 471 source-training and 139 target-adaptation samples found that source-fitted scaling places 17.61% of target-adaptation shared-gene values outside `[0,1]` (maximum 39.90; 99th percentile 4.64). A separate MinMax fit on target-adaptation samples maps those same adaptation rows into `[0,1]`; the median target/source per-gene range ratio is 2.22 (10th–90th percentiles 0.42–6.97). This supports a controlled target-adaptation-only scaling candidate, not adoption: it changes intensity semantics and no classifier performance has been measured. Once the current fingerprinted run chain completes, compare this policy against source-fitted scaling with identical patient splits and classifier settings. Fit the candidate scaler on target adaptation only, then freeze it before transforming target-test patients; do not inspect test labels to select the policy.

## Retraining and performance checks

The original seed-46 all-feature comparison used the earlier percentile-threshold filter and has no source-tree/environment fingerprints; its positive KPCA-DA ΔBA of 0.167 is historical and not directly comparable to current runs. The fingerprinted deterministic-rank seed-46 pair is now complete and is the current-policy result: baseline accuracy/BA/macro-F1 were 0.410/0.597/0.410; KPCA-DA scored 0.344/0.331/0.313. Normal sensitivity fell 0.923→0.308, while Tumor sensitivity rose 0.271→0.354. The paired ΔBA was −0.266, with a conditional patient-cluster 95% interval of −0.442 to −0.065. Both arms share the corrected cohort hashes, patient split, classifier configuration, source-tree fingerprint and environment-lock fingerprint. The adapted run selected λ=0.1 and γ=7.838. This is clear evidence that the current KPCA-DA configuration harms balanced performance on this development split; because the older positive result lacks code provenance, the difference cannot be attributed solely to the one-gene filter-count change. See [the fingerprinted comparison](full_tcga_seed46_ranked_variance_comparison.json).

The clipped seed-46 and seed-47 pairs have both completed. On seed 47, KPCA-DA accuracy/macro-F1 rose from 0.468/0.464 to 0.548/0.524, while balanced accuracy changed from 0.635 to 0.630 (ΔBA −0.005; conditional patient-bootstrap interval −0.192–0.181). Normal sensitivity fell from 0.923 to 0.769 and Tumor sensitivity rose from 0.347 to 0.490. Across the two clipped seeds, BA improved on one and declined on the other; mean ΔBA is 0.022. Both conditional ΔBA intervals include zero, and Normal sensitivity declined on both seeds. Clipping is not established as a balanced-performance improvement despite its accuracy and macro-F1 gains. The seed-46 and seed-47 comparisons are [here](full_tcga_seed46_official_all_clipped_comparison.json) and [here](full_tcga_seed47_official_all_clipped_comparison.json); the verified same-configuration summary is [here](tvsn_official_all_clipped_seed_summary.json). Both pairs selected λ=0.1; automatic γ differed by seed and clipping policy, so this comparison evaluates each complete preprocessing/selection policy rather than isolating clipping alone.

The seed-47 unclipped pair has completed. Accuracy rose from 0.226 to 0.548 and macro-F1 from 0.196 to 0.410, but balanced accuracy fell from 0.510 to 0.403. Normal sensitivity dropped from 1.000 to 0.154 as Tumor sensitivity rose from 0.020 to 0.653. Across the two matched unclipped seeds, KPCA-DA improved accuracy and macro-F1 on both splits, but balanced accuracy improved on only one; the mean ΔBA is 0.030 (sample SD 0.193). Mean Normal sensitivity change is −0.423, while mean Tumor sensitivity change is +0.483. These two development splits therefore show a strong class tradeoff and do not establish a robust balanced-class improvement. The verified descriptive aggregation, which checks the effective adaptation objective as well as cohort and common settings, is [tvsn_official_all_seed_summary.json](tvsn_official_all_seed_summary.json). It is not an estimate of generalization to new patients or cohorts.
